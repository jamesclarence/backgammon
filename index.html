    Instructions

    Opponent. Play 2 Players to share the keyboard/mouse with someone else (hot-seat), or vs Computer to play solo against a built-in opponent that plays Black. Switch anytime with the buttons at the top — it starts a new game.

    Modes. Beginner mode shows a running tip and a strategy note above the dice that explain what to do and why. Normal mode hides those for a cleaner screen once you know the ropes.

    Goal. Race all 15 of your checkers around the board and off before your opponent does.

    Direction. White moves from point 24 down to point 1, then bears off. Black moves from point 1 up to point 24, then bears off. This is a hot-seat game — two players share one screen, taking turns.

    Turns. Click Roll dice. You'll get two numbers (four if you roll doubles). Click a die to select it, then click a highlighted checker to move it that many points.

    Blocked points. You can't land on a point held by two or more of your opponent's checkers.

    Hitting. Landing on a single opposing checker (a "blot") sends it to the bar.

    The bar. If you have a checker on the bar, you must re-enter it in your opponent's home board before making any other move.

    Bearing off. Once all 15 of your checkers are in your home board (points 1–6 for White, 19–24 for Black), you can start bearing them off with a die matching the exact point, or a higher die if no checkers sit further back.

    Winner. First player to bear off all 15 checkers wins.

    Strategy basics

    • Safety first. A checker alone on a point (a "blot") can be hit and sent all the way back to the bar. Move blots to a point with another of your own checkers when you can.
    • Make key points. Two or more checkers on one point form a "made point" your opponent can't land on. Your 5-point (point 5 for White, point 20 for Black) and the bar-point are especially valuable.
    • Build primes. A row of made points in sequence forms a "prime," which can trap enemy checkers behind it entirely.
    • Know the race. Roughly compare how far each side's checkers have left to travel. Ahead in the race? Run your checkers home and avoid risks. Behind? Playing it safe rarely wins — look for chances to hit instead.
    • Anchors. If you're behind, a made point deep in your opponent's home board gives you a safe base and a chance at a well-timed hit later.
    • Bear off efficiently. Clear your farthest-back point first when you have a choice, so a lucky opponent roll can't punish a straggler.
